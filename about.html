About Us

Bay Area AI Navigator compares major AI developments at Stanford, in Silicon Valley and in San Francisco on one timeline. It is a fictional coursework site and is not affiliated with any organisation named on it.

What gets listed

An item is listed only when it is a major development, is tied to a specific place in one of the three areas, links to its source and shows when it was last checked. Company announcements are labelled as self-reported, and research preprints are labelled as not peer reviewed.

How items are scored

Each item gets an assessed strength out of 100 and a relevance score for each of the three areas. The scores are a judgement made against a published rubric, not a measurement. The rubric is shown on the home page under the timeline.

How it is kept up to date

The data lives in one file, developments.json. A GitHub Actions workflow is scheduled to run every hour (GitHub treats scheduled runs as best effort, so some are late or skipped), reads a list of news and institution feeds (sources.json), keeps stories that are about AI and name Stanford, Silicon Valley or San Francisco, scores them against the rubric and commits the result. This page reads the file again every five minutes.

Items added this way are labelled as assessed by automated rules. A script can match places and score by type of source, but it cannot check whether a story supports its headline, so those items are not hand-checked. For them, “last verified” means the date the feed was read.
